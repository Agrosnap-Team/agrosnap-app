Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)
1. Overview & Impact
Pathogen: Begomovirus coheni (TYLCV). A dicot-infecting begomovirus with two main strains: mild (detected in northern Victoria) and severe (infects both beans and tomatoes).

Impact: Can cause devastating yield losses of up to 100% in both field and glasshouse-grown tomatoes.

Vector: Exclusively transmitted by the Silverleaf Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci), which is now considered established in Victoria. It is not known to be seed-transmitted or mechanically transmitted in Australia.

2. Visual Symptoms
Tomatoes:

Leaves become noticeably smaller and yellow between the veins (interveinal chlorosis).

Leaf margins curl upwards and inwards towards the center.

Seedlings experience shortened shoots, giving them a stunted, bushy look.

New growth on mature plants is significantly reduced in size.

Note: Overall tomato production drops severely, but existing fruit usually remains visually unaffected.

Common Beans (Severe Strain):

Leaflets exhibit yellow mosaic patterning, crumpling, thickening, and upward/downward curling.

Causes abnormal growth/proliferation in apical and side shoots.

3. Host Range & Epidemiology
Primary Hosts: Tomatoes, Capsicums (glasshouse), Common Beans (Phaseolus vulgaris), Petunias, and Lisianthus.

Weed/Alternative Hosts: Thorn apple (shows symptoms) and Marshmallow (symptomless carrier).

Patterns: Typically spreads randomly or in distinct patches across a crop. Infection severity is strongly tied to local Silverleaf Whitefly population sizes.



Diagnostic Lookalikes (Differential Diagnosis)
To prevent misidentification in your project, compare TYLCV against these lookalikes:

Tomato Big Bud: Results in abnormally enlarged buds and green flower structures.

Tomato Yellow Top Virus: Leaves are down-curled or up-curled but are specifically rounded and reduced in size.

Physiological Leaf Roll (Water Stress): Leaves roll but remain soft and pliable (not rigid), and the plant is not stunted.

Phosphate Deficiency: The entire plant develops a distinct purplish tinge, accompanied by stiff, stunted growth.

Magnesium Deficiency: Interveinal yellowing is confined mostly to the middle and lower (older) leaves.
