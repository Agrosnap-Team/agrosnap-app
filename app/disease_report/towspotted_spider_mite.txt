Two-Spotted Mite
Common Name: Two-spotted mite / Two-spotted spider mite

Scientific Name: Tetranychus urticae

Status: Cosmopolitan priority pest (found worldwide, including all of Australia)

1. Identification & Physical Description
Eggs: Minute (~0.14 mm), round, transparent when laid, turning pale yellow-white later.

Larvae/Nymphs: 6-legged pale larvae hatch first. After feeding, they turn yellow/green and develop two distinct dark body spots. They mature through 8-legged protonymph and deuteronymph stages.

Adult Females: ~0.5 mm long, oval, rounded rear. Summer coloration is typically yellowish-green with two prominent dark spots on either side of the body. Can change to orange, reddish-brown, or black depending on the host crop.

Adult Males: Smaller (~0.3 mm), narrower, and pointed at the rear. Light yellow to dark brown; body spots are less conspicuous.

Key Feature: Both sexes possess two distinct reddish eyespots. Mostly found on the underside of leaves.

2. Lifecycle & Behavior
Speed: Lifecycle can be as short as 2 weeks in hot, dry weather.

Reproduction: Females can lay up to 6 eggs per day (70+ lifetime). Unfertilized eggs yield only males; fertilized eggs yield both sexes.

Overwintering: Triggered by short days and low temperatures. Adult females turn orange-red, stop feeding/laying, and hibernate under lower tree bark or ground debris. They emerge and lose red color in spring. Glasshouse populations do not hibernate.

Webbing: Active stages spin profuse silk webbing to protect themselves from predators and chemical sprays.

3. Crop Damage & Hosts
Host Range: Extremely wide generalist feeder (targets many crops and ornamentals).

Symptoms: Nymphs and adults pierce cells to suck contents, creating chlorotic (yellowish) spots that give leaves a "stippled" look. Heavy infestations cause leaves to turn yellow or bronzed, shrivel, and drop prematurely.

High-Risk Triggers: Highly destructive in hot, dry weather. Plants near dust sources (like dirt roads) are highly susceptible.

Specific Risk (Pears): Pear trees are highly sensitive. Premature summer leaf drop can trigger out-of-season flowering, ruining the next year's crop buds.

4. Management & Control
Chemical Issues: High risk of miticide resistance. Broad-spectrum insecticides kill native beneficial insects and worsen outbreaks.

Biological Control (Predatory Mites):

Phytoseiulus persimilis (Chilean predatory mite) – widely used in horticultural crops.

Neoseiulus californicus – highly tolerant of hot Australian fruit-growing climates.

Galendromus occidentalis – successfully used in Integrated Pest Management (IPM) for Victorian apple, pear, and peach orchards.

Stethorus (Small black ladybird beetles) – natural predators, but usually arrive only after mite numbers are already high.
